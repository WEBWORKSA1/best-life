Health: the energy that pays for everything else

Sleep, movement and food in the order that gives the biggest return for the least effort.

By the Best.Life editorial team · 3 min read · Reviewed 2026-11-19

Key takeaways

  • Sleep is the first lever: fix wake time before anything else.
  • Walking counts. Most of the benefit of exercise arrives in the first 150 minutes a week.
  • Eat mostly plants and protein you recognise; consistency beats any named diet.

Every other area of life is paid for in energy. A sharp mind, patient parenting, a demanding career and a good mood all draw from the same account, and that account is filled by three unglamorous deposits: sleep, movement and food. People tend to attack them in the reverse order of usefulness — starting with an ambitious diet, adding a punishing gym plan, and treating sleep as what is left over. Flip the order.

1. Sleep first

Most adults function well on seven to nine hours. Fewer than seven on a regular basis is associated with worse mood, weaker attention and poorer long-term health. The single most effective habit is not an earlier bedtime but a fixed wake-up time, seven days a week. Your body clock anchors to when you get up and see light; a stable morning makes you sleepy at a predictable hour at night.

  • Get outdoor light within an hour of waking, even on a cloudy day.
  • Stop caffeine about eight hours before bed.
  • Keep the bedroom cool, dark and boring. Charge the phone in another room.

2. Move often, lift sometimes

Global health guidance for adults converges on roughly 150 minutes of moderate activity a week — a brisk half-hour walk on five days — plus two sessions that challenge your muscles. The curve is steep at the start: going from nothing to a little delivers more benefit than going from a lot to slightly more. If you sit for work, the walk after lunch is the highest-value appointment in your calendar.

Strength matters more with every decade. Two short sessions a week of pushing, pulling, squatting and carrying is enough to keep muscle and bone, and with them your independence later in life.

3. Eat like an adult who plans to be around

Nutrition research is noisy, but the stable findings are boring: eat vegetables, fruit, beans, whole grains and nuts often; get enough protein; drink mostly water; keep ultra-processed snacks and sugary drinks occasional. You do not need a named diet. You need a default breakfast, a default lunch and three dinners you can cook without thinking.

A 14-day starter

  1. Days 1–14: same wake-up time every day.
  2. Days 3–14: ten-minute walk after your largest meal.
  3. Days 7–14: one extra portion of vegetables at dinner.

That is all. Resist adding more until these feel automatic. Then use the Life Score again and watch what moved.

General information only. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or are starting exercise after a long break, speak with a qualified health professional first.
