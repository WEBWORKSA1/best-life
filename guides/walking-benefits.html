Walking is underrated: what a daily walk does for body and mind

Why the humble walk may be the highest-return health habit — and how to fit one in.

By the Best.Life editorial team · 2 min read · Reviewed 2026-11-19

Walking has no equipment, no learning curve and almost no injury risk, which is probably why it is taken less seriously than it deserves.

What regular walking is linked to

  • Better heart health and blood-sugar control, especially when you walk after meals.
  • Improved mood and lower stress within a single session.
  • Better sleep, particularly when the walk is outdoors in daylight.
  • Clearer thinking — many people find ideas come more easily on the move.

How much?

You do not need a specific step count. Benefits rise steadily from low levels, and a brisk thirty minutes on most days meets common activity guidelines. "Brisk" means you can talk but not sing.

Fitting it in

  • Ten minutes after each main meal adds up to thirty.
  • Take phone calls on foot.
  • Get off one stop early or park at the far end.
  • Make one weekly catch-up with a friend a walk instead of a coffee.

Start where you are. If ten minutes is your current limit, ten minutes is the plan.
